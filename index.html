Research project · Pavel Dubov

How does the physical world emerge?

From pre-vacuum and local distinctions to events, a stable vacuum, propagating waves and matter. We investigate which minimal laws could genuinely lead to observable physics.

Research program ↓What remains to be proven
01 / The starting question

Do not postulate a ready-made world — investigate its emergence

We examine possible mathematical structures from which local events and a physical vacuum might emerge. Symmetry and consistency of possible states alone do not demonstrate that events actually occur or that physical dynamics exist.

PRE-VACUUM

Before familiar physical quantities

What minimal relations and rules are admissible without presupposing space, time or particles?

LOCAL DISTINCTION

From possibility to event

What distinguishes an allowed change from an actually realized transition? How might causal order arise?

PHYSICAL VACUUM

Stability and excitations

Can a single dynamical law yield a stable ground state and propagating excitations?

02 / Research framework

From structure to testable dynamics

Each stage must follow from explicitly stated assumptions. An appealing analogy or parameter tuning cannot substitute for deriving the next stage.

01 — Structure

Define admissible states and local distinctions.

02 — Histories

Specify a law for actual transitions, events and internal ordering.

03 — Vacuum

Derive a stable state and an operator of linear response.

04 — Physics

Test waves, shared causality and possible matter excitations.

Status: This is a research program, not a proven theory of the origin of the Universe. Mathematical consistency alone does not establish physical realizability or experimental confirmation.
03 / Physical validation

What a candidate underlying law must satisfy

Rather than tuning separate speeds and properties, we seek their joint origin in one structure and its response operator.

CRITERION A

Stable vacuum

Test the state under admissible perturbations and check for unphysical unstable modes.

CRITERION B

Waves and energy

Test dynamical energy transport and the appearance of two transverse massless modes in an appropriate limit.

CRITERION C

A common causal cone

Different sectors should inherit a common causal geometry from the same law, rather than acquire matching speeds through manual adjustment.
